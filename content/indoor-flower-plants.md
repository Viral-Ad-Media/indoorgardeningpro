Foliage plants are easy, but nothing brightens a room like flowers. These houseplants bloom indoors without a greenhouse. Most need one thing above all: **more light than leafy plants**. A bright windowsill is usually the key to getting flowers.

## 1. African violet (Saintpaulia)

![African violet in bloom](/uploads/2022/04/african-violet.jpg)

Compact plants with velvety leaves and purple, pink or white flowers almost all year round. Give them bright, indirect light and water from below with room-temperature water, keeping the leaves dry.

## 2. Anthurium

![Red anthurium flowers](/uploads/2022/04/anthurium.jpg)

Glossy, heart-shaped "flowers" (actually colourful spathes) that last for weeks. Anthuriums like bright, indirect light, warmth and humidity. Water when the top few centimetres of soil are dry.

## 3. Peace lily (Spathiphyllum)

White flowers above dark green leaves, and one of the few bloomers that tolerates lower light. It flowers best in medium light. See [low-light indoor plants](/best-low-light-indoor-plants/).

## 4. Christmas cactus (Schlumbergera)

![Christmas cactus flowers](/uploads/2022/04/christmas-cactus.jpg)

A forest cactus, not a desert one, so it likes more water than most cacti. To trigger flowers, give it cool nights and around 12–14 hours of darkness each night for six weeks in autumn. Don't move it once buds form, or they may drop.

## 5. Kalanchoe

![Kalanchoe flowers](/uploads/2022/04/kalanchoe.jpg)

A succulent with clusters of small, bright flowers that last for weeks. It needs bright light and should dry out between waterings. Like the Christmas cactus, it reblooms after a period of long nights.

## 6. Moth orchid (Phalaenopsis)

The easiest orchid for homes. Grow it in bark mix, not soil, in bright, indirect light. Water when the roots turn silvery, and let it drain completely. A slight drop in night temperature in autumn helps it send up a new flower spike.

## 7. Bromeliads

![Bromeliad with a red bract](/uploads/2022/04/bromeliads.jpg)

Bold, long-lasting colour from a rosette of stiff leaves. Each rosette flowers once, then produces "pups" that you can grow on. Keep the soil lightly moist and give bright, indirect light.

## 8. Cyclamen

![Pink cyclamen](/uploads/2022/04/cyclamen.jpg)

A winter bloomer with swept-back petals and marbled leaves. Cyclamen likes cool rooms, around 10–18°C (50–65°F), and bright light. Water from below, avoiding the crown. It goes dormant in summer, which is normal.

## 9. Clivia

![Orange clivia flowers](/uploads/2022/04/clivia.jpg)

Clusters of orange trumpet flowers in late winter or spring. It is tough and long-lived and flowers best when slightly pot-bound. A cool, dry rest in autumn encourages blooms.

## 10. Crown of thorns (Euphorbia milii)

![Crown of thorns flowers](/uploads/2022/04/crown-of-thorns.jpg)

A spiny succulent that flowers almost continuously in a sunny window. It handles dry air and infrequent watering. Its sap is irritating, so wear gloves when handling it.

## How to get houseplants to flower

- **Give more light.** This is the number one reason indoor plants don't bloom.
- **Feed with a fertilizer suited to flowering plants** during the growing season. See [indoor plant fertilizer](/indoor-plant-fertilizer/).
- **Respect rest periods.** Many bloomers need a cooler or darker spell to set buds.
- **Don't over-pot.** Several flowering plants bloom better when slightly root-bound.
- **Deadhead** faded flowers to keep the plant tidy and encourage more.

## Pet safety

Kalanchoe, cyclamen, clivia, peace lily, anthurium and crown of thorns are toxic to pets. African violets, moth orchids, bromeliads and Christmas cactus are generally considered pet-safe.
