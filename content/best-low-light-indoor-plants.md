Every home has a corner that doesn't get much sun: a hallway, a bathroom with frosted glass, an office far from the window. The good news is that plenty of plants evolved on shady forest floors and are perfectly happy there.

One thing to know first: **low light is not no light.** If you can comfortably read a book in the spot during the day without switching on a lamp, most of the plants below will cope. A windowless room needs a grow light.

## The one mistake to avoid

Plants in low light grow slowly, so they use water slowly. Overwatering is by far the most common way people kill low-light plants. Always let the top few centimetres of soil dry out before watering, and use a pot with a drainage hole.

## 1. Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)

![Pothos trailing from a pot](/uploads/2022/05/pothos.jpg)

The classic beginner plant. Pothos trails or climbs, tolerates dim light, and tells you when it's thirsty by drooping slightly. Variegated types lose some of their pattern in very low light, but stay healthy.

## 2. Snake plant (Dracaena trifasciata)

Upright, architectural and nearly indestructible. Snake plants can go weeks without water and handle low light, though they grow fastest in bright, indirect light. See our full [snake plant care guide](/how-to-care-for-snake-plants/).

## 3. ZZ plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)

Glossy leaves and thick underground rhizomes that store water. ZZ plants shrug off neglect and dim light. Water only when the soil is completely dry.

## 4. Peace lily (Spathiphyllum)

![Peace lily with white flowers](/uploads/2022/04/peace-lily.jpeg)

One of the few plants that will flower in fairly low light. Peace lilies droop dramatically when dry and perk up within hours of watering, which makes them easy to read.

## 5. Cast iron plant (Aspidistra elatior)

Named for its toughness. It handles deep shade, cool rooms and irregular watering. It grows slowly, so buy one at the size you want.

## 6. Heartleaf philodendron (Philodendron hederaceum)

Similar to pothos, with softer, heart-shaped leaves. It trails beautifully from a shelf and is very forgiving.

## 7. Chinese evergreen (Aglaonema)

Patterned leaves in green, silver, pink or red. Darker green varieties handle low light best; the brightly coloured ones prefer a bit more.

## 8. Parlor palm (Chamaedorea elegans)

A small, graceful palm that has been grown indoors since Victorian times. It likes even moisture and indirect light.

## 9. Prayer plant (Maranta leuconeura)

![Prayer plant leaves](/uploads/2022/05/prayer-plant.jpg)

Its patterned leaves fold up at night like hands in prayer. It likes moderate humidity and soil that stays lightly moist, not wet.

## 10. Ferns

Boston ferns and button ferns are naturally shade plants. They need more humidity than most on this list, so they do well in bathrooms. See our guide to [humidity for indoor plants](/humidity-for-your-indoor-plants/).

## Quick care tips for dim rooms

- **Water less often** than you would in a bright room.
- **Dust the leaves** every few weeks so they can use every bit of light.
- **Rotate the pot** a quarter turn every week or two so growth stays even. More in [how often to rotate indoor plants](/how-often-should-you-rotate-indoor-plants/).
- **Skip heavy feeding.** Fertilize lightly in spring and summer only.
- **Move plants closer to the window in winter**, when light levels drop.

## Pet safety

Several popular low-light plants, including pothos, philodendron, peace lily, ZZ plant, snake plant and Chinese evergreen, are toxic to cats and dogs if chewed. If you have pets, the parlor palm, cast iron plant, prayer plant and Boston fern are generally considered safer choices.
