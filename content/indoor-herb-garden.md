An indoor herb garden is one of the most rewarding things you can grow. It takes up very little space, saves money, and puts fresh flavour within arm's reach of the stove.

## The secret: light

Most culinary herbs come from sunny places. Indoors, they need **at least six hours of bright light a day**, which means a south- or west-facing window, or a grow light. A dim kitchen will give you weak, leggy herbs with little flavour.

If your windows aren't bright enough, a small LED grow light 15–30 cm above the herbs for 12–14 hours a day makes a huge difference. See [the best indoor plant light](/best-indoor-plant-light/).

## The best herbs to grow indoors

### Basil

![Basil plant](/uploads/2022/04/basil.jpg)

Loves warmth and bright light. Keep it away from cold windows in winter. Pinch off the top pair of leaves regularly so it branches out, and remove flower buds to keep the leaves tasty.

### Mint

![Mint in a pot](/uploads/2022/04/mint.jpg)

Grows vigorously and tolerates a little less light than most herbs. Always give mint its own pot, because it will crowd out anything it shares with.

### Chives

![Chives](/uploads/2022/04/chives.jpg)

Easy and productive. Snip leaves from the outside, about 2–3 cm above the soil, and they'll regrow.

### Parsley

![Parsley](/uploads/2022/04/parsley.jpg)

Slow to germinate from seed, so buy a young plant. It likes a deep pot and evenly moist soil.

### Thyme

![Thyme](/uploads/2022/04/thyme.jpg)

Woody and drought-tolerant. Give it full sun and let the soil dry between waterings.

### Rosemary

![Rosemary](/uploads/2022/04/rosemary.jpg)

Needs the brightest spot you have and good drainage. It dislikes both soggy soil and drying out completely, so water when the top few centimetres are dry.

### Oregano

![Oregano](/uploads/2022/04/oregano.jpg)

Hardy and easy, with a stronger flavour when grown in bright light and kept slightly on the dry side.

### Sage, tarragon, chervil and bay

These also grow well indoors. Sage and bay like sun and dry conditions; chervil prefers a cooler, slightly shadier spot.

## Pots and soil

- Use pots with drainage holes. Herbs hate wet feet.
- Use a light, free-draining potting mix. For rosemary, thyme and sage, mix in some perlite or grit.
- Group herbs with similar needs: Mediterranean herbs (rosemary, thyme, sage, oregano) together on the dry side; basil, parsley, mint and chives together on the moist side.

## Watering

Check the soil with your finger. Water when the top 2–3 cm is dry, until water runs out of the bottom. Empty the saucer afterwards. Mediterranean herbs can dry out a little more between waterings.

## Harvesting for a steady supply

- **Harvest little and often.** Regular picking encourages new growth.
- **Never take more than a third** of the plant at once.
- **Cut just above a pair of leaves**, which encourages two new shoots to grow.
- **Pinch out flowers** on basil, mint and oregano to keep leaf flavour strong.

## Feeding

Herbs don't need much. A half-strength liquid fertilizer every four weeks in spring and summer is plenty. Overfeeding produces lush growth with weaker flavour. See [indoor plant fertilizer](/indoor-plant-fertilizer/).

## Troubleshooting

- **Leggy, pale growth:** more light needed.
- **Yellow lower leaves:** usually overwatering.
- **Tiny flies around the soil:** fungus gnats from wet soil. See [preventing indoor plant pests](/prevent-indoor-plant-pests/).
- **Supermarket basil dying quickly:** those pots are crowded with many seedlings. Split the clump into three or four smaller pots and they'll last much longer.
