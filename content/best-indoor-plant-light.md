Light is the single biggest factor in whether an indoor plant thrives or just survives. Water, soil and fertilizer all matter, but a plant that doesn't get enough light can't use any of them well. The good news is that getting light right is mostly about a few simple choices.

## Start with the light you already have

Before you buy anything, look at your windows.

- **South-facing windows** (in the Northern Hemisphere) get the most light all day. Great for herbs, succulents, cacti and fruiting plants.
- **East-facing windows** get gentle morning sun. Ideal for most leafy houseplants, African violets and ferns.
- **West-facing windows** get strong, hot afternoon sun. Good for sun-lovers, but watch for scorched leaves in summer.
- **North-facing windows** get soft, indirect light. Best for low-light plants such as pothos, snake plants and ZZ plants.

Light falls off quickly as you move away from glass. A plant two metres from a window may get less than a quarter of the light it would get on the sill. If a plant is leaning hard toward the window, stretching out with long gaps between leaves, or losing its lower leaves, it wants more light.

## When you need a grow light

A grow light makes sense when:

- You want to grow herbs, vegetables or flowering plants indoors year-round.
- Your home has small or shaded windows.
- Winter days are short where you live.
- You are starting seeds, which need strong light to avoid getting leggy.

## Types of grow light

![LED grow light over seedlings](/uploads/2022/04/LED-grow-light.jpg)

### LED grow lights

LEDs are the best choice for almost every home gardener. They use little electricity, run cool enough to sit fairly close to leaves, and last for years. "Full-spectrum" white LEDs look natural in a living room and give plants everything they need. Red and blue "blurple" panels work too, but the purple glow is hard to live with.

### Fluorescent lights

![Fluorescent tubes over a seed tray](/uploads/2022/04/fluorescent-light.jpg)

T5 fluorescent tubes were the standard for seed starting for decades and still work well for seedlings, leafy greens and herbs. They are cheap to buy but use more power than LEDs and need replacing every year or two as their output fades.

### Incandescent and halogen bulbs

Skip them. They give off mostly heat, not useful light, and can scorch leaves.

### HID lights

High-intensity discharge lights (metal halide and high-pressure sodium) are powerful but hot, power-hungry and overkill for a home setup. Modern LEDs have replaced them for most growers.

## How close and how long

| Plant type | Distance from LED | Hours per day |
|---|---|---|
| Seedlings | 15–30 cm (6–12 in) | 14–16 |
| Herbs and leafy greens | 20–40 cm (8–16 in) | 12–16 |
| Fruiting plants (tomatoes, peppers) | 20–30 cm (8–12 in) | 14–16 |
| Foliage houseplants | 30–60 cm (12–24 in) | 10–12 |

These are starting points. Always check the maker's guidance, because power varies widely between lights. Plants also need darkness to rest, so don't leave lights on 24 hours a day. A cheap plug-in timer makes this effortless.

## Signs the light is wrong

**Too little light:** pale leaves, long stretched stems, slow growth, no flowers, leaning toward the window.

**Too much light:** bleached or white patches, crispy brown edges, leaves curling away from the light.

If you see either, adjust the distance first, then the hours.

## What to look for when buying

- **Full spectrum** output, ideally described as suitable for both growth and flowering.
- **A real wattage figure.** Many cheap lights quote an inflated "equivalent" wattage. The actual power draw is a better guide.
- **Adjustable height** or a stand, so you can raise the light as plants grow.
- **A built-in timer** or compatibility with a plug-in one.
- **Coverage area** that matches your shelf or tray.

## The bottom line

For most people, a full-spectrum LED on a timer, placed 20–40 cm above the plants for 12–16 hours a day, is all you need. Start with the light from your windows, add a grow light where it falls short, and watch how your plants respond.
