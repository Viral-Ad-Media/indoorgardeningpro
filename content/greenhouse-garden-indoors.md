You don't need a backyard to have a greenhouse. A small indoor greenhouse, sometimes called a plant cabinet or grow tent, creates a pocket of warm, humid air that helps seeds sprout, cuttings root and humidity-loving plants thrive, all inside your home.

## Why use a greenhouse indoors?

Homes are usually dry, especially in winter when the heating is on. Many plants, particularly seedlings and tropical species, prefer 50–70% humidity, while a heated room can drop below 30%. A greenhouse traps moisture and warmth around the plants, giving you:

- **Faster, more reliable seed germination**
- **Better rooting of cuttings**
- **Healthier tropical plants** such as calatheas, ferns and orchids
- **A head start on the outdoor season**, with transplants ready to plant out in spring

## Types of indoor greenhouse

![Portable greenhouse with zip-up cover](/uploads/2022/04/portable-greenhouse.jpeg)

### Tabletop and windowsill domes

Clear plastic lids that sit over a seed tray. Cheap, simple and perfect for starting seeds. Many come with adjustable vents.

### Mini shelf greenhouses

Two to four shelves in a metal frame with a clear zip-up cover. They hold a lot of plants in a small footprint and work well near a bright window or with grow lights clipped to each shelf.

### Walk-in or tall greenhouses

Larger frames for a spare room, garage or sunroom. Useful if you grow many seedlings each spring.

### Glass cabinets

A popular option for collectors of tropical plants. A glass display cabinet fitted with LED lights and a small fan makes a beautiful, humid home for plants that struggle in normal rooms.

## Setting it up

1. **Choose the spot.** Near a bright window is ideal, but avoid strong direct afternoon sun, which can overheat a closed greenhouse fast.
2. **Add light if needed.** On a shelf greenhouse, fix a slim LED grow light under each shelf. See [choosing the best indoor plant light](/best-indoor-plant-light/).
3. **Add a thermometer and hygrometer.** A cheap digital one tells you the temperature and humidity at a glance.
4. **Plan for airflow.** Still, damp air encourages mould and fungus gnats. Open the vents or unzip the door for a while each day, or add a small clip-on fan.
5. **Use a tray.** Put a waterproof tray on each shelf to catch drips and protect your floor.

## Ideal conditions

| | Seed starting | Tropical plants |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 20–27°C (68–80°F) | 18–27°C (65–80°F) |
| Humidity | 70–90% until germination, then lower | 50–70% |
| Light | 14–16 hrs under grow lights | Bright, indirect |

A seedling heat mat under the tray speeds up germination for warm-season crops such as tomatoes and peppers.

## Common problems

- **Mould on the soil surface:** too humid and too little airflow. Ventilate more and water less.
- **Leggy seedlings:** not enough light. Lower the grow light or add more hours.
- **Wilting on hot days:** the greenhouse is overheating. Move it out of direct sun or open it fully.
- **Fungus gnats:** soil staying too wet. Let the top layer dry out and see [how to prevent indoor plant pests](/prevent-indoor-plant-pests/).
