Snake plants (*Dracaena trifasciata*, formerly *Sansevieria*) are famous for being hard to kill. Their stiff, upright leaves store water, they tolerate low light, and they don't mind dry air. Most of their care comes down to one rule: **water less than you think.**

## Light

Snake plants are flexible:

- **Best:** bright, indirect light. They grow faster and keep stronger leaf patterns.
- **Tolerated:** low light, such as a north-facing room or a hallway. Growth will be very slow.
- **Also fine:** some direct sun, once the plant is used to it. Introduce it gradually to avoid scorch.

In low light, a snake plant uses very little water, so cut back watering even further.

## Watering: the most important part

Overwatering causes almost every snake plant death. The roots and the base of the leaves rot in soggy soil.

- **Let the soil dry out completely** between waterings. Push a finger or a wooden skewer deep into the pot to check.
- **Spring and summer:** typically every two to four weeks.
- **Autumn and winter:** often every four to eight weeks, sometimes less.
- **Water around the edge of the pot**, not into the centre of the leaf rosette, and let it drain fully.
- **Never let the pot stand in water.**

If in doubt, wait another week. A thirsty snake plant shows slightly wrinkled or curling leaves and recovers quickly once watered. An overwatered one may not recover.

## Soil and pot

- Use a **free-draining cactus or succulent mix**, or regular potting mix with a generous amount of perlite or grit added.
- **Terracotta pots** are ideal because they let soil dry out faster.
- Always use a pot with a **drainage hole**.

## Temperature and humidity

Snake plants are comfortable at normal room temperatures, roughly 15–29°C (60–85°F). Keep them away from cold draughts and windows in winter; temperatures below about 10°C (50°F) can damage leaves. Normal household humidity is fine.

## Feeding

Snake plants need very little. Use a diluted cactus or balanced fertilizer once a month in spring and summer at most, and none in winter. See [indoor plant fertilizer](/indoor-plant-fertilizer/).

## Repotting

They like to be snug in their pots and only need repotting every two to three years, or when roots crack a plastic pot or poke out of the bottom. Go up just one pot size, in spring. See [how to repot indoor plants without stress](/repot-indoor-plants-without-stress/).

## Propagating

Snake plants are easy to multiply:

- **Division:** when repotting, split the clump into sections, each with roots and a few leaves, and pot them separately. This is the fastest method.
- **Leaf cuttings:** cut a leaf into 5–8 cm sections, let them dry for a day or two, then push the bottom end (the end that was nearest the soil) into moist cactus mix. They root slowly, over several weeks to months. Note that variegated yellow edges usually don't carry over to plants grown from leaf cuttings.

## Common problems

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soft, mushy leaves at the base | Overwatering, root rot | Remove from pot, cut away rotten parts, repot in dry mix, water much less |
| Wrinkled, curling leaves | Underwatering | Water thoroughly, then return to normal routine |
| Leaves falling over | Overwatering or too little light | Check roots; move to brighter spot |
| Brown, crispy tips | Inconsistent watering or cold damage | Keep conditions steady |
| Pale, stretched new growth | Too little light | Move closer to a window |

## Is it pet-safe?

No. Snake plants are mildly toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, causing stomach upset. Keep them out of reach of pets that like to nibble.

## The takeaway

Bright, indirect light, gritty soil, a pot with drainage, and water only when it's completely dry. Do that and your snake plant can live for decades. For more easy plants, see our list of the [easiest houseplants for beginners](/easiest-houseplants-for-beginners/).
