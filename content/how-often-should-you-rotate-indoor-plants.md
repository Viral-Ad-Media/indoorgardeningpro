If your houseplant has started leaning toward the window like it's trying to escape, it isn't broken. It's doing exactly what plants do: growing toward the light. Rotating the pot is the simplest way to keep it straight, full and balanced.

## Why plants lean

Plants grow toward light through a process called phototropism. Cells on the shaded side of a stem grow a little faster than cells on the lit side, which bends the stem toward the light. Indoors, light usually comes from one direction, so over weeks a plant becomes lopsided: lush on the window side and sparse on the room side.

## How often to rotate

**For most houseplants, a quarter turn (90°) every one to two weeks is ideal.**

An easy habit is to rotate each plant when you water it, always turning in the same direction. That way every side gets equal time facing the light.

Adjust for your situation:

- **Bright, one-sided light** (a plant right next to a sunny window): every week.
- **Moderate light:** every two weeks.
- **Low light or light from several sides:** once a month is usually enough.
- **Under a grow light directly overhead:** rarely needed at all.

## Plants that benefit most

- **Fiddle-leaf figs and rubber plants:** prone to leaning and losing their lower leaves on the shaded side.
- **Monstera and philodendrons:** large leaves turn to face the light and can make the plant look one-sided.
- **Snake plants and ZZ plants:** grow slowly, but will lean over time.
- **Succulents and cacti:** stretch quickly toward light. Regular turns keep them compact.
- **Herbs on a windowsill:** basil and similar herbs bend toward the glass within days.

## When not to rotate

Some plants dislike being moved, and turning them can cause them to drop buds or flowers.

- **Plants in bud or flower,** such as Christmas cactus, gardenias and orchids. Leave them in place until flowering finishes.
- **Newly repotted plants.** Give them a couple of weeks to settle.
- **Plants recovering from stress** after a move, pest treatment or a cold draught.

## Common mistakes

**Turning too far.** A half turn swaps the shaded and sunny sides all at once. Leaves that were angled toward the light suddenly face away and may take time to readjust. Quarter turns are gentler.

**Rotating instead of fixing the light.** If a plant is leaning badly even with regular turns, it probably needs more light overall. Move it closer to the window or add a grow light. See [the best indoor plant light](/best-indoor-plant-light/).

**Forgetting which way you turned.** Always turn in the same direction (for example, clockwise). Some people put a small sticker on one side of the pot as a marker.

**Moving the plant to a different spot.** Rotating means turning it in place. Moving it to a completely new spot with different light is a bigger change and can cause leaf drop.

## Fixing a plant that already leans

1. Start rotating it a quarter turn each week.
2. For tall plants, add a stake and loosely tie the stem to it.
3. Prune any long, stretched stems to encourage bushier growth from lower down.
4. Be patient. It may take a whole growing season to straighten out.

## The takeaway

Rotate most indoor plants a quarter turn every one to two weeks, ideally when you water. It takes seconds and gives you fuller, straighter, healthier plants.
