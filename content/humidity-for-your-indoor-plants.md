Many popular houseplants come from tropical forests where the air is warm and moist. Our homes, especially with heating or air conditioning running, are often as dry as a desert. If your plants have brown, crispy leaf tips or edges, low humidity is a likely cause.

## What humidity do houseplants need?

Relative humidity (RH) is how much moisture the air holds compared with the maximum it could hold at that temperature.

| Plant group | Comfortable humidity | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Desert plants | 20–40% | Cacti, succulents, snake plant, ZZ plant |
| Most houseplants | 40–50% | Pothos, philodendron, monstera, rubber plant |
| Humidity lovers | 50–70%+ | Calathea, ferns, prayer plant, orchids, nerve plant |

A typical heated home in winter sits around 20–35%, which is fine for cacti but uncomfortable for ferns.

## How to measure it

Buy an inexpensive digital hygrometer. They cost very little and take the guesswork out. Place it near your plants, not on a radiator or next to a humidifier, and check it at different times of day.

## Signs of low humidity

- Brown, crispy leaf tips and edges
- New leaves that are stunted or fail to unfurl
- Flower buds that dry up and drop
- Spider mites, which thrive in dry air

Note that brown tips can also come from underwatering, fertilizer build-up or tap water chemicals, so check those too.

## Ways to raise humidity, ranked

### 1. Use a humidifier (most effective)

![A humidifier near houseplants](/uploads/2022/04/humidifier.jpeg)

A room humidifier is the only method that reliably raises humidity across a whole space. Cool-mist ultrasonic models are quiet and popular. Clean it regularly following the manufacturer's instructions, and use distilled or filtered water if your tap water is hard, to avoid white mineral dust.

### 2. Group plants together

Plants release moisture through their leaves. Clustering them creates a slightly more humid pocket of air. It's free and it looks good.

### 3. Choose the right room

Bathrooms and kitchens are naturally more humid. A bright bathroom is an excellent home for ferns and other humidity lovers.

### 4. Use a greenhouse or cabinet

For the fussiest plants, an enclosed space works best. See [setting up a greenhouse garden indoors](/greenhouse-garden-indoors/). Terrariums are another great option: see [terrarium plants](/indoor-garden-terrarium-plants/).

### 5. Pebble trays (small effect)

Fill a tray with pebbles, add water to just below the top of the pebbles, and set the pot on top. As the water evaporates it adds a little moisture right around the plant. Make sure the pot isn't sitting in water.

### 6. Misting (very small effect)

![Spraying a houseplant with water](/uploads/2022/04/spraying-indoor-plant-to-increase-humidity.jpg)

Misting raises humidity for only a few minutes. It can help clean dust off leaves, but it won't fix a dry room. Avoid misting fuzzy-leaved plants such as African violets, as water on the leaves can cause spots.

## Other tips

- **Keep plants away from heaters, radiators and air-conditioning vents.** These blast out very dry air.
- **Avoid cold draughts** near doors and single-glazed windows in winter.
- **Don't overdo it.** Very high humidity with poor airflow encourages mould and fungal diseases. Keep air moving with an open door or a small fan.

## The takeaway

Buy a hygrometer, find out where you stand, and match your plants to your rooms. For humidity lovers in a dry home, a humidifier or an enclosed space makes the biggest difference.
