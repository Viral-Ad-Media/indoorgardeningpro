Repotting gives a plant fresh soil and room to grow, but done carelessly it can cause wilting, yellow leaves and a long sulk. A little preparation makes all the difference.

## Does your plant need repotting?

Look for these signs:

- Roots growing out of the drainage hole or circling the surface
- Water running straight through the pot without soaking in
- The plant drying out within a day or two of watering
- The plant looking top-heavy or tipping over
- Growth slowing in spring and summer despite good care
- It has been more than two years since the last repot

Many plants are happy being slightly snug, so don't repot just because it's been a year.

## When to repot

**Early spring to early summer** is best, when plants are entering active growth and can recover quickly. Avoid repotting in winter, while a plant is flowering, or right after bringing a new plant home. Give new plants two to three weeks to adjust first.

## Choosing the new pot

- **Go up only one size**: 2–5 cm (1–2 inches) wider than the old pot. A pot that's too big holds excess wet soil around the roots and invites rot.
- **Drainage holes are essential.**
- **Terracotta** dries out faster, which suits succulents and plants prone to overwatering.
- **Plastic or glazed ceramic** holds moisture longer, which suits thirstier plants.

## What you need

- New pot and saucer
- Fresh potting mix suited to the plant. See [indoor plant soil and compost](/indoor-plant-soil-and-compost/).
- A repotting mat, tray or newspaper to keep things tidy
- Clean scissors or snips
- Gloves (useful for prickly or irritant plants)

## Step-by-step

1. **Water the day before.** Moist roots slide out more easily and handle disturbance better than dry ones.
2. **Remove the plant.** Turn the pot on its side, support the base of the stems between your fingers, and ease it out. Squeeze plastic pots or run a knife around the inside edge if it's stuck.
3. **Inspect the roots.** Healthy roots are firm and white or tan. Trim any that are black, mushy or smell bad.
4. **Loosen circling roots.** Gently tease apart roots that wind around the root ball so they'll grow outward into new soil. For a very tight root ball, make a few shallow vertical cuts.
5. **Add soil to the new pot.** Put enough fresh mix in the bottom so the top of the root ball will sit about 2 cm below the rim.
6. **Position and fill.** Centre the plant, fill around it with fresh mix, and firm gently. Don't pack it down hard. Keep the plant at the same depth it was before.
7. **Water thoroughly** and let it drain completely.
8. **Place it back in its usual spot**, out of harsh direct sun for a week or so while it settles.

## Aftercare: avoiding transplant shock

- **Don't fertilize for four to six weeks.** Fresh mix contains nutrients, and new roots are tender.
- **Keep watering normal.** Check the soil before watering as usual; resist the urge to overwater a droopy plant.
- **Keep it out of extremes** of heat, cold and strong sun.
- **Expect a little drooping or a yellow leaf or two.** It should recover within a couple of weeks.

## Refreshing without a bigger pot

If a plant is already as big as you want it, you can root-prune and repot it into the same pot. Trim up to a quarter of the outer roots, refresh the soil, and return it to its pot. This keeps it the same size while giving it new soil.

## The takeaway

Repot in spring, go up only one pot size, keep the plant at the same depth, and give it a few quiet weeks afterwards. Your plant will settle in and grow on strongly.
