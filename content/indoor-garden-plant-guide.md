Starting an indoor garden is easier than most people think. You don't need a big home or a green thumb. You need a decent spot, a few suitable plants and a simple routine. This guide walks you through it.

## Step 1: Decide what you want to grow

Your goal shapes everything else.

- **Greenery and decoration:** start with easy foliage houseplants such as pothos, snake plants and spider plants.
- **Fresh herbs:** basil, mint, chives and parsley on a sunny windowsill. See our [indoor herb garden guide](/indoor-herb-garden/).
- **Food:** salad leaves, microgreens and compact vegetables. See [indoor vegetable plants](/indoor-vegetable-plants/).
- **Flowers:** African violets, orchids, kalanchoe and more. See [indoor flower plants](/indoor-flower-plants/).

## Step 2: Find your light

Light decides which plants will do well. Spend a day noticing how much sun each window gets.

- **Bright, direct sun** (south or west windows): herbs, vegetables, succulents, cacti.
- **Bright, indirect light** (east windows or near a sunny window): most houseplants and flowering plants.
- **Low light** (north windows or further into the room): low-light specialists. See [the best low-light indoor plants](/best-low-light-indoor-plants/).

If your light is limited, a grow light opens up far more options. Read [how to choose the best indoor plant light](/best-indoor-plant-light/).

## Step 3: Gather the basics

You need less than garden centres would have you believe:

- **Pots with drainage holes.** This matters more than anything else on this list.
- **Saucers or trays** to catch water.
- **Good potting mix** suited to your plants. See [indoor plant soil and compost](/indoor-plant-soil-and-compost/).
- **A watering can with a narrow spout.** See [indoor plant watering cans](/indoor-plant-watering-cans/).
- **A balanced fertilizer** for the growing season.
- **Optional:** small pruning snips, a moisture meter and a grow light.

## Step 4: Choose easy plants to start

Begin with forgiving plants so you build confidence. Good first choices:

1. Pothos
2. Snake plant
3. Spider plant
4. ZZ plant
5. Peace lily
6. Basil or mint (for a sunny window)

See our full list of the [easiest houseplants for beginners](/easiest-houseplants-for-beginners/).

## Step 5: Learn to water properly

More indoor plants die from overwatering than anything else. The rule of thumb:

- Push a finger into the soil. If the top 2–3 cm (about an inch) is dry, water.
- Water thoroughly until it runs out of the drainage hole.
- Empty the saucer after 15 minutes so the pot doesn't sit in water.

Succulents and cacti want to dry out completely; ferns and humidity lovers want to stay lightly moist.

## Step 6: Build a simple weekly routine

Once a week, spend ten minutes on your plants:

- Check soil moisture and water those that need it.
- Turn each pot a quarter turn. See [how often to rotate indoor plants](/how-often-should-you-rotate-indoor-plants/).
- Look under leaves for pests. See [how to prevent indoor plant pests](/prevent-indoor-plant-pests/).
- Remove yellow or dead leaves.
- In spring and summer, feed according to the fertilizer label.

## Step 7: Grow your garden over time

Once your first plants are thriving, try something new: a [terrarium](/indoor-garden-terrarium-plants/), a [hydroponic system](/hydroponic-garden-indoors/) or a mini [indoor greenhouse](/greenhouse-garden-indoors/). Repot plants as they outgrow their pots, using our guide to [repotting indoor plants without stress](/repot-indoor-plants-without-stress/).

## Beginner mistakes to avoid

- Buying plants for how they look without checking their light needs
- Using pots with no drainage hole
- Watering on a fixed schedule instead of checking the soil
- Repotting into a pot far too big
- Putting plants next to heaters or cold draughts

Start small, pay attention, and add plants as you learn. That's the whole secret.
