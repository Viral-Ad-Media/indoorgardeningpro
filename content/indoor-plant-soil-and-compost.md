Soil is a plant's home, pantry and water tank. For indoor plants, the right potting mix needs to hold enough moisture, drain freely, and let air reach the roots. Get it wrong and even the healthiest plant will struggle.

## Why you shouldn't use garden soil

Soil dug from the garden compacts in a pot, drains poorly and can bring in pests, weed seeds and diseases. Indoor plants need a **potting mix**: a lighter, sterile blend designed for containers.

## What's in a good potting mix

Most potting mixes combine a few of these ingredients:

- **Peat moss or coco coir:** hold moisture and give the mix body. Coco coir is a more sustainable alternative to peat.
- **Perlite:** white, lightweight volcanic granules that improve drainage and air flow.
- **Vermiculite:** holds water and nutrients; useful for seed starting.
- **Pine bark:** chunky material that improves drainage, used in mixes for orchids and aroids.
- **Compost or worm castings:** add organic matter and gentle nutrients.
- **Sand or grit:** improve drainage for succulents and cacti.

## Matching the mix to the plant

| Plants | Mix |
|---|---|
| Most houseplants | All-purpose indoor potting mix |
| Succulents and cacti | Cactus mix, or potting mix with 30–50% perlite or grit |
| Orchids | Bark-based orchid mix, never regular soil |
| Monstera, philodendron, pothos | Potting mix with added bark and perlite for a chunky, airy blend |
| African violets | Light, peat- or coir-based African violet mix |
| Herbs and vegetables | Rich potting mix with compost |
| Seeds | Fine seed-starting mix |

## Where compost fits in

![Compost in hands](/uploads/2022/05/compost.jpg)

Compost is decomposed organic matter. It improves soil structure, feeds beneficial microbes and releases nutrients slowly. For indoor plants:

- **Use it as an ingredient, not on its own.** Pure compost is too dense and stays too wet in a pot. Around 10–25% of the mix is plenty.
- **It's great for edible plants** such as herbs and vegetables, which are hungrier than most houseplants.
- **Worm castings** are a gentle, low-odour option for indoor use. A thin top-dressing in spring gives plants a boost.

### Benefits of soil and compost done right

![Benefits of soil and compost](/uploads/2022/05/benefit-of-soil-and-compost.jpg)

- Healthier roots thanks to better air and water balance
- Slower, steadier nutrient release
- Fewer problems with root rot and fungus gnats
- Less need for synthetic fertilizer

## Can you compost indoors?

![Composting kitchen waste](/uploads/2022/05/composting-waste.jpg)

Yes. A small worm bin (vermicomposting) or a sealed bokashi bin can turn kitchen scraps into rich material for your plants with little smell. Fruit and vegetable peels, coffee grounds and crushed eggshells all work well.

A note on home "hacks": banana peels and coffee grounds are fine *in compost*, but burying them directly in a houseplant pot can attract pests and grow mould. Compost them first.

## When to refresh the soil

Potting mix breaks down over time, becoming compacted and less able to drain. Refresh it:

- **Every one to two years** for most houseplants, when you repot. See [how to repot indoor plants without stress](/repot-indoor-plants-without-stress/).
- **When water runs straight through** or sits on top without soaking in.
- **When you see a white crust** of mineral salts on the surface.

## The takeaway

Buy a quality potting mix suited to your plant, add perlite or bark for plants that like drainage, and use compost as a nutritious ingredient rather than the whole recipe.
