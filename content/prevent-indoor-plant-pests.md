Even the best-kept indoor plants can pick up pests. They arrive on new plants, through open windows or on cut flowers. The good news: if you catch them early, most are easy to deal with.

## Prevention: the five habits that matter most

1. **Quarantine new plants** for two to three weeks, away from your other plants, and inspect them closely before they join the collection.
2. **Check plants weekly**, especially the undersides of leaves and where leaves meet stems.
3. **Don't overwater.** Constantly wet soil invites fungus gnats and root rot.
4. **Keep leaves clean.** Wipe them with a damp cloth every few weeks. It removes dust and dislodges early pests.
5. **Keep humidity sensible.** Very dry air encourages spider mites. See [humidity for indoor plants](/humidity-for-your-indoor-plants/).

## The common indoor plant pests

### Spider mites

![Spider mite damage on a leaf](/uploads/2022/04/spider-mites.jpg)

Tiny, barely visible mites that cause fine yellow speckling on leaves and, in bad cases, delicate webbing. They thrive in warm, dry air.

**Treat:** rinse the plant thoroughly in the shower, raise humidity, and spray with insecticidal soap or horticultural oil every five to seven days for a few weeks.

### Fungus gnats

![Fungus gnats on a sticky trap](/uploads/2022/04/fungus-gnats.jpg)

Small black flies that hover around the soil. The adults are harmless but annoying; the larvae live in wet soil and can damage roots of seedlings.

**Treat:** let the top few centimetres of soil dry out between waterings, use yellow sticky traps to catch adults, and consider a soil drench containing *Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis* (BTI) to kill larvae.

### Mealybugs

![Mealybugs on a stem](/uploads/2022/04/mealybugs.jpg)

White, cottony clusters in leaf joints and along stems. They suck sap and leave sticky honeydew.

**Treat:** dab individual bugs with a cotton bud dipped in rubbing alcohol, then spray with insecticidal soap. Repeat weekly.

### Aphids

![Aphids on a plant](/uploads/2022/04/aphids.jpg)

Small green, black or white insects clustered on new growth and buds.

**Treat:** wash them off with water, then use insecticidal soap. They're easy to control if caught early.

### Scale insects

![Scale insects on a stem](/uploads/2022/04/scales.jpg)

Small brown or tan bumps on stems and leaves that don't look like insects at all. They leave sticky residue.

**Treat:** scrape them off gently with a fingernail or soft brush, then apply horticultural oil. Persistent.

### Thrips

![Thrips damage](/uploads/2022/04/thrips.jpg)

Slender insects that cause silvery streaks and black specks on leaves.

**Treat:** rinse the plant, use blue sticky traps, and spray with insecticidal soap or neem oil regularly.

### Whiteflies

![Whiteflies under a leaf](/uploads/2022/04/whiteflies.jpg)

Tiny white flying insects that rise in a cloud when you disturb the plant.

**Treat:** yellow sticky traps plus insecticidal soap sprayed on the undersides of leaves.

### Springtails

![Springtails in soil](/uploads/2022/04/springtails.jpg)

Tiny insects that jump on the soil surface after watering. They feed on decaying matter and are harmless to plants; they simply indicate damp soil.

## Safe treatment basics

- **Isolate** the affected plant straight away.
- **Start gentle:** water sprays and wiping before stronger products.
- **Insecticidal soap and horticultural oils** work on contact, so coat all surfaces, especially leaf undersides.
- **Repeat treatments.** Most products don't kill eggs, so treat every five to seven days for at least three weeks.
- **Test first** on a single leaf and wait a day, as some plants are sensitive.
- **Always follow the product label** and keep products away from children and pets.
- **Know when to give up.** A badly infested plant may be better discarded than risking the rest of your collection.

## The takeaway

Inspect new plants, check your collection weekly, and avoid soggy soil. Most pest problems are small when you catch them early.
