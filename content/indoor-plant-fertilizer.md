Plants in the ground can send roots far and wide to find nutrients. Plants in pots can't. Over a few months they use up the nutrients in their potting mix, and without feeding they slowly become pale and stop growing. The right fertilizer, used sparingly, keeps them healthy.

## Reading the label: N-P-K

Every fertilizer shows three numbers, such as 10-10-10 or 3-1-2. They give the percentage of:

- **N, nitrogen:** leafy, green growth.
- **P, phosphorus:** roots and flowers.
- **K, potassium:** overall health, disease resistance, flowers and fruit.

For most foliage houseplants, a **balanced** fertilizer (similar numbers, or slightly higher nitrogen) works well. Flowering and fruiting plants benefit from more potassium once they start to bloom.

## Types of fertilizer

### Liquid fertilizer

![Liquid fertilizer](/uploads/2022/04/liquid-fertilizer.jpg)

Mixed into water when you water. Fast-acting and easy to control. Most popular for houseplants.

### Slow-release granules or spikes

![Slow-release fertilizer](/uploads/2022/04/slow-release-fertilizer.jpg)

Coated granules or spikes that release nutrients gradually over weeks or months. Convenient, but harder to adjust, and they can over-concentrate around a spike.

### Granular fertilizer

![Granular fertilizer](/uploads/2022/04/granular-fertilizer.jpg)

Sprinkled on the soil surface and watered in. More common outdoors; use sparingly in pots.

### Organic fertilizer

![Organic fertilizer](/uploads/2022/04/organic-fertilizer.jpg)

Made from natural sources such as seaweed, fish emulsion, worm castings or compost. Gentler and less likely to burn roots, though some (like fish emulsion) smell strongly indoors.

## When to feed

- **Spring and summer:** plants are actively growing, so feed regularly.
- **Autumn:** reduce feeding as growth slows.
- **Winter:** stop or feed very rarely, unless the plant is growing strongly under grow lights.
- **Newly repotted plants:** wait four to six weeks; fresh potting mix already contains nutrients.
- **Sick or stressed plants:** don't feed until they recover. Fertilizer isn't medicine.

## How much and how often

A safe approach for most houseplants:

- **Liquid feed at half the label strength, every two to four weeks** in the growing season.
- **Hungry plants** such as vegetables, herbs and fast-growing tropicals may need feeding every one to two weeks.
- **Succulents and cacti** need very little: a diluted feed once a month in summer is plenty.

When in doubt, use less. Underfeeding slows growth; overfeeding can kill.

## Signs of overfeeding

- White crust on the soil surface or pot rim
- Brown, burnt leaf tips and edges
- Wilting even though the soil is moist
- Sudden leaf drop

**Fix it** by flushing the pot: run plenty of plain water through the soil several times and let it drain fully, then skip feeding for a month or two.

## Signs of underfeeding

- Pale or yellowing older leaves
- Small new leaves
- Very slow growth during spring and summer

## Tips for success

- **Always water before feeding** if the soil is bone dry, so fertilizer doesn't scorch dry roots.
- **Flush pots every few months** to prevent salt build-up.
- **Match the feed to the plant:** balanced for foliage, higher potassium for flowers and fruit.
- **Don't rely on kitchen hacks.** Coffee grounds and banana peels are better in the compost bin than directly in a pot. See [indoor plant soil and compost](/indoor-plant-soil-and-compost/).
