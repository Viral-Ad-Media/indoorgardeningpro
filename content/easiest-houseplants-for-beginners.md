"Easy" houseplants still die. Usually it's one specific mistake, and it's different for each plant. Here are twelve of the most forgiving houseplants, with the thing most likely to kill each one.

## 1. Pothos

**Why it's easy:** grows in almost any light, trails beautifully, and droops visibly when it's thirsty.

**What kills it:** overwatering in low light. Let the top few centimetres of soil dry out first.

## 2. Snake plant

**Why it's easy:** tolerates low light, dry air and weeks of neglect.

**What kills it:** too much water, especially in winter. Its roots rot easily. Read our full [snake plant care guide](/how-to-care-for-snake-plants/).

## 3. ZZ plant

**Why it's easy:** stores water in underground rhizomes and handles low light.

**What kills it:** watering on a schedule. Water only when the soil is completely dry, which may be every three to four weeks.

## 4. Spider plant

**Why it's easy:** fast-growing, adaptable, and produces baby plants you can pot up.

**What kills it:** rarely dies, but brown tips come from dry air, fluoride in tap water or letting it dry out too far.

## 5. Peace lily

**Why it's easy:** flowers in modest light and wilts dramatically when thirsty, so it tells you what it needs.

**What kills it:** being left wilted too often. Repeated drying out damages roots. Also dislikes cold draughts.

## 6. Heartleaf philodendron

**Why it's easy:** like pothos, it handles a range of light and is quick to recover from mistakes.

**What kills it:** soggy soil and very cold rooms.

## 7. Chinese evergreen (Aglaonema)

**Why it's easy:** attractive patterned leaves and tolerance of lower light.

**What kills it:** cold. It dislikes temperatures below about 15°C (60°F) and cold windows in winter.

## 8. Rubber plant (Ficus elastica)

**Why it's easy:** sturdy glossy leaves and steady growth in bright, indirect light.

**What kills it:** being moved around. Like many figs, it drops leaves when its light or location changes suddenly.

## 9. Cast iron plant

**Why it's easy:** handles deep shade, cool rooms and irregular care.

**What kills it:** almost nothing, except waterlogged soil.

## 10. Jade plant

![Jade plant](/uploads/2022/05/jade-plants.jpg)

**Why it's easy:** a succulent that stores water in its thick leaves and lives for decades.

**What kills it:** low light (it gets leggy and weak) and overwatering. Give it your sunniest window and let it dry out between waterings.

## 11. Aloe vera

**Why it's easy:** a drought-tolerant succulent that needs little attention.

**What kills it:** too little light and too much water. Use a cactus mix and a terracotta pot.

## 12. Parlor palm

**Why it's easy:** a compact palm that's happy in medium to low light and is generally considered pet-safe.

**What kills it:** dry soil for too long and very dry air, which causes brown tips and spider mites.

## Three rules that save almost every beginner plant

1. **Check the soil before you water.** Never water on a fixed calendar.
2. **Use pots with drainage holes.** Water that can't escape rots roots.
3. **Match the plant to your light.** See [the best low-light indoor plants](/best-low-light-indoor-plants/) for dim rooms and [indoor plant light](/best-indoor-plant-light/) for brighter options.

## Pet safety

Pothos, snake plant, ZZ plant, peace lily, philodendron, Chinese evergreen, rubber plant, jade plant and aloe vera are toxic to pets if eaten. Spider plants, cast iron plants and parlor palms are generally considered safe.
