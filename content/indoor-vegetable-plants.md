Growing vegetables indoors is very doable, as long as you pick the right crops and give them enough light. Leafy greens and compact plants do well; sprawling crops like pumpkins and corn belong outdoors.

## Light comes first

Vegetables need much more light than houseplants. A very sunny south-facing window can work for leafy greens, but for fruiting crops like tomatoes and peppers you will almost certainly need a grow light running 14–16 hours a day. See [the best indoor plant light](/best-indoor-plant-light/).

## Easiest: leafy greens

### Lettuce and salad leaves

![Lettuce growing in a container](/uploads/2022/04/lettuce.jpg)

Fast, compact and tolerant of slightly lower light. Grow loose-leaf varieties and pick the outer leaves as you need them; the plant keeps producing for weeks.

### Spinach, kale and Swiss chard

Harvest young leaves for salads or let them grow larger for cooking. They prefer cooler rooms.

### Microgreens

The quickest crop of all, ready in one to three weeks. See our guide to [microgreens and sprouts](/microgreens-and-sprouts/).

## Easy: regrowing and onion family

### Spring onions (scallions)

![Scallions](/uploads/2022/04/scallions.jpg)

Stand the white root ends from shop-bought spring onions in a little water or plant them in soil, and they'll regrow green tops you can snip repeatedly.

### Garlic greens

![Garlic greens](/uploads/2022/04/garlic-greens.jpg)

Plant individual garlic cloves close together in a pot. You won't get bulbs indoors, but you'll get mild garlic-flavoured shoots in a couple of weeks.

## Moderate: root vegetables

### Carrots

![Carrots](/uploads/2022/04/carrots.jpg)

Choose short, round or stubby varieties and grow them in a deep pot (at least 25–30 cm) of loose potting mix. Thin seedlings so each has space.

### Radishes

Quick (around four weeks), compact and great for a sunny windowsill or under lights.

### Potatoes

![Potatoes](/uploads/2022/04/potatoes.jpg)

Possible in a large container or grow bag in a bright spot, but they take a lot of space for a modest harvest. A fun project rather than a practical one.

## Harder: fruiting crops

These need strong light, warmth and hand pollination, but they're very satisfying.

### Cherry tomatoes

![Tomatoes](/uploads/2022/04/tomatoes.jpg)

Pick a compact "dwarf" or "micro" variety bred for containers. Give it 14–16 hours of strong light, a pot of at least 10–20 litres, and support. When it flowers, gently shake the stems or tap the flowers each day to help pollination.

### Chilli peppers

![Hot peppers](/uploads/2022/04/hot-pepper.webp)

Chillies are well suited to indoor growing. They like warmth and bright light, and compact varieties stay small. Hand-pollinate with a small paintbrush.

## Containers and soil

- Use pots with drainage holes, sized to the crop: small for herbs and greens, large for tomatoes.
- Use a rich potting mix with some compost. See [indoor plant soil and compost](/indoor-plant-soil-and-compost/).
- Vertical planters and tiered stands make good use of limited space.

## Feeding and watering

- Vegetables are hungry. Feed every one to two weeks during active growth with a balanced liquid fertilizer, or a high-potassium feed once fruiting crops start flowering. See [indoor plant fertilizer](/indoor-plant-fertilizer/).
- Keep soil evenly moist but never waterlogged. Containers dry out faster than garden beds, especially under grow lights.

## Common problems

- **Leggy, pale seedlings:** not enough light.
- **Flowers but no fruit:** pollination isn't happening. Hand-pollinate.
- **Aphids and whiteflies:** check under leaves regularly. See [preventing indoor plant pests](/prevent-indoor-plant-pests/).

## Want to skip the soil?

Leafy greens and herbs grow especially fast in a [hydroponic system](/hydroponic-garden-indoors/), which can be a cleaner option for a kitchen counter.
